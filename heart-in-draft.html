Heart in Draft

Prom Night

notes

Heart in Draft is a collection of poems that got out of hand.

They were written over years, on legal pads and in the Notes app and once, allegedly, on the back of a motion for summary judgment. They were meant to stay on the page. Then I discovered you could hand a poem to a machine and get a song back, and I did that roughly a thousand times, and these sixteen are the ones that survived. The machine sang; the words are mine. Every chorus I could keep, I kept; every chorus I couldn't, I fought until it surrendered.

What they're about is the usual: love found and love endured, the people you lose and the ones you erase on purpose, a rooster in Key West, a bar on St. Patrick's Day, a kitchen where two careful people learn a language for what they can't say. There's a lot of water in here — creeks, tides, a boat named after a siren. There's a lot of leaving, and a lot of not-leaving. If the record has an argument, it's that the wanting to go and the choosing to stay are the same weather.

I make no claims about the singer, who is a machine and does not exist, except in the eighth-rests, where I'd like to think something does.

The words are drafts. The heart is real. Mostly.

This is dedicated to: Hannah, Emerson, Dot, and Scott.
Your lives are the best poems I know.

— Prom Night
